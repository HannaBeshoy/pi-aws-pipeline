# Cost estimate

Generated by `python3 tools/cost.py` (run it again with `--devices` and `--interval` for other cases). List prices for us-east-1, the AWS Academy region, as published in November 2026, **without** the free tier; check them in the [AWS Pricing Calculator](https://calculator.aws/) before relying on them. Assumptions: 5 readings a second per device (the Arduino node's rate), 30-day months, the Lambda function runs 0.4 s at 256 MB (arm64) per file, raw data kept 12 months (the lifecycle rule), DynamoDB summaries kept 90 days (TTL).

The size of a reading is measured, not guessed: a simulated hour is written as the uploader writes it and compressed. Real readings are noisier than the simulation and will compress less, so the S3 lines are a lower bound; they are a few cents either way.

## What the numbers say

- **Storing every reading in DynamoDB would cost about 12 times as much** (USD 10.91 against 0.92 a month for one device), and almost all of it is write requests: 13 million writes a month against 52 thousand. That is why the Lambda function writes one summary item per minute and the raw readings stay in S3, where 13 million readings are 0.11 GB and a few PUT requests. Raw data can still be analysed later from S3 (for example with Amazon Athena) when it is needed.
- **CloudWatch is the largest item** for one device: each custom metric costs USD 0.30 a month whatever the traffic. The function sends two (Readings and BadReadings) instead of one per quantity; both fall within the free tier of 10 metrics.
- **Uploading every 60 s instead of 300 s** multiplies the requests and Lambda invocations by five; it matters from about ten devices up. 300 s is the default: the readings reach the cloud at most about six minutes late, which is fine for a temperature log, and the Pi's own TCP port still gives the live value.
- With the free tier, one device costs almost nothing beyond the two CloudWatch metrics and alarms, which are also within the free allowance.
